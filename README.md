# NCAA Financial Analysis Report  
## Lamar University Athletics (FY20–FY24)

📊 Comprehensive financial analysis of Lamar University Athletics using five years of NCAA Financial Reporting System (FRS) data. This project evaluates revenue trends, expense structures, institutional dependency, and sport-level financial performance to identify key insights that may support long-term athletic budgeting and operational planning. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## 📌 Project Overview

This analysis was conducted using NCAA Financial Reporting System (FRS) data from FY20–FY24 provided by Lamar University Athletics. The project focused on understanding:

- Revenue and expense trends across five fiscal years
- Institutional dependency and revenue composition
- Sport-by-sport financial sustainability
- Department-level financial structure
- Key operational cost drivers and long-term sustainability

The analysis showed that Lamar University Athletics operates under a highly subsidized financial model in which institutional support plays a dominant role, while meaningful self-generated revenue is concentrated primarily in football and a small number of sports. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

# 📂 Project Components

## 1️⃣ Overall Financial Trend Analysis

Analyzed five years of:
- Total Revenue
- Total Expenses
- Net Operating Performance

### Key Findings
- Revenue and expenses moved closely together across FY20–FY24
- The department consistently operated near break-even
- COVID-19 disruptions impacted FY21, followed by recovery beginning in FY22
- Financial growth in FY23–FY24 was primarily allocation-driven rather than generated internally

📈 Visualizations Included:
- Revenue vs Expense Trend
- Year-over-Year Revenue and Expense Growth

---

## 2️⃣ Revenue Composition Analysis

Revenue was separated into:

### Allocated Revenue
- Student Fees
- Direct Institutional Support
- Indirect Institutional Support
- Facilities Debt / Lease Support

### Generated Revenue
- Ticket Sales
- Sponsorships
- Contributions
- Guarantees
- Camps
- Program / Parking / Concessions

### Key Findings
- Allocated revenue consistently represented the majority of total revenue
- Institutional dependency increased over time
- Generated revenue remained relatively limited and volatile
- Recent growth was driven primarily by institutional allocations

📊 Visualizations Included:
- Allocated vs Generated Revenue ($)
- Revenue Composition (%)

---

## 3️⃣ Expense Structure Analysis

Expenses were grouped into major operational categories:

- Personnel Costs
- Athletic Student Aid
- Facilities
- Team Travel
- Equipment & Uniforms
- Operations

### Key Findings
- Personnel and student aid consistently represented the largest expense categories
- Expense structure remained stable across all five years
- The department operates with a largely fixed or semi-fixed cost structure
- Limited short-term flexibility exists due to high personnel and facility costs

📊 Visualization Included:
- Expense Structure Composition (%)

---

## 4️⃣ Sport-by-Sport Financial Analysis (FY24)

Conducted detailed sport-level analysis to evaluate:

- Generated Revenue
- Allocated Revenue
- Revenue Dependence
- Institutional Support by Sport

### Key Findings
- Football generated the highest percentage of its own revenue (~31%)
- Men’s Basketball and Baseball showed moderate generation capacity
- Most sports generated less than 10% of total revenue independently
- Several sports relied on institutional support for more than 90% of revenue

📊 Visualizations Included:
- % Generated Revenue by Sport
- Revenue Composition by Sport
- Sport-Level Revenue Dependence

---

## 5️⃣ Sport-Level Trend Comparison (FY23 vs FY24)

Compared major sports across FY23 and FY24 to identify short-term structural shifts.

### Key Findings
- Football increased generated revenue share significantly
- Men’s Basketball became more allocation dependent
- Baseball remained relatively stable
- Women’s Soccer showed modest improvement
- Overall financial structure remained highly subsidized

📊 Visualization Included:
- FY23 vs FY24 Generated Revenue Comparison

---

## 6️⃣ Department-Level Financial Structure (FY24)

Performed a department-level breakdown of revenue and expense structure.

### Key Findings
- Approximately 75–76% of revenue came from institutional sources
- Student fees contributed more than 23% of total revenue
- Ticket sales represented a negligible portion of revenue
- Salaries and facilities represented the dominant expense categories
- Spending patterns reflected a support-driven operational model rather than a profit-driven model

📊 Visualizations Included:
- Department Revenue Composition
- Department Expense Structure

# 📈 Major Insights

- Lamar University Athletics operates under a highly institution-supported financial model
- Financial sustainability currently depends heavily on institutional funding
- Revenue generation is concentrated primarily in football
- Expense structure is stable but dominated by fixed operational costs
- Most sports are not financially self-sustaining
- The department appears operationally stable but structurally dependent on continued institutional support
